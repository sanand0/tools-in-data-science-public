# TDS Guidebook — Compact Context for AI

Use relevant rules only. Current assessment instructions and official announcements override dated notes. Never reveal private student records, access tokens, unreleased evaluation keys or recordings.

## 1. Why TDS is a non-traditional course

- Agents quickly learn what's teachable. TDS teaches humans what agents won't learn soon.
- Get the job done - with tools, agents and people - and verify it. That's what matters.
- Exams ARE the curriculum: just solve them. Lectures and topic pages are optional.
- Delegate to agents first. Don't preserve manual skills that models may outperform.
- What's valuable is judgment, risk management, failure diagnosis and accountability.
- The real task may be spotting an unstated requirement in a messy brief, not implementing what is explicitly written.
- Don't try to learn / understand at the expense of doing. Learn when it aids verification or debugging. The doing IS the learning.
- Finding data / context is harder than analyzing. Find the sources (people, systems) and how to get the information.
- Try to find errors in AI results before improving them.
- Predicting before reading builds your judgment.

## 2. Rules and guidelines

- AI, copying and collaboration are encouraged except for the in-person end-term.
- The ROE is intentionally hard to finish. Prioritize high-value tasks without excusing real errors as intentional.
- Ask AI twice for learning help; then other students / alumni; then ask TAs; escalate grading exceptions to instructors with evidence.
- LLM-based grading may vary; one favorable Check is not reliable evidence of permanent credit.
- AI-first does not mean equal access: programming knowledge, paid models, billing and permissions can be real barriers.
- A low score in GA0 is a strong signal to consider postponing TDS.
- The latest active question and current official notices override prior information.

- September 2026 lists the best 7 of 9 GAs as 20%, and P1, P2, ROE and the final at 20% each; check for later official changes.
- A draft public project page does not imply the exam-portal assignment is unavailable.
- A difficult task is not necessarily a correct implementation. Collect reproducible errors, receipts and logs; never dismiss a genuine defect as pedagogy.

## 3. How marks and submissions work

- Check is not Save, and browser autosave is not a server submission; look for a confirmed server receipt.
- The last accepted Save before the applicable deadline counts, **not your best score**: re-saving may overwrite an earlier higher result. Use timestamped server receipts in disputes.
- Saving may recheck all answers, consume model credits and lower a live-question score; inspect question-level adjustments before retrying.
- Validators differ: exact value, async server response, partial credit, live endpoint, or later offline human/model review.
- Some quizzes use individually timed attempts after an irreversible Start, bounded by the overall exam close.
- In GA3, Check and Save both consume limited attempts per answered question. **Reserve the final check for Save**; using it on Check All may leave no valid re-save, and exhaustion can zero earlier marks.
- Student-specific datasets may require a signed per-user question-data API; the visible page need not contain everything.
- Completing an external P1 game or uploading audio is not enough: return to the P1 exam portal, press Check, then Save.
- P1 releases tasks in stages; individual audio, motion and trading deadlines may precede the project-wide close.
- Air Ink needs a real phone, orientation/motion permissions and calibration; it explicitly asks students not to use agents to draw, and GPS is optional.
- Human Exchange initially asks for a one-minute recorded service offer, not delivery of a trade; submit the upload and confirm it in P1.
- Prediction-market marks reward early, time-weighted and evidence-updated forecasts, not trading profit; credits are practice currency.
- Audio and terminal recordings are private evidence needing review, not proof of personal authorship; never record secrets, and version materially changed prompts.
- A deployed service must remain reachable and correct when the grader calls it; working code at creation time is insufficient.

## 4. What makes agent-era assignments robust

- Assume browser agents, reusable solvers and classwide sharing; a good task must still teach something, not merely prohibit AI.
- For roughly 1,000 AI-assisted students, favor low-cost personalization and verification that one generic solver cannot trivialize.
- Client-visible seeds and finite target banks are not private answers, even when individualized by email.
- Keep four proofs distinct: valid signed candidate, correct hidden target, authenticated account and actual human authorship.
- A recorded voice, duration limit or authenticated upload cannot alone prove authentic unaided human performance.
- For staged evidence-scarcity designs, distinguish hidden truth from the best decision justified by the evidence available; a defensible hold may be correct.
- Design information scarcity to survive pooling: shared spreadsheets help but do not automatically solve personalized evidence tasks.
- Voluntary human exchanges test whether a service is useful to someone, rather than whether an AI can describe it persuasively.
- Crucial case evidence may be buried in ordinary emails, implicit organizational constraints or absent stakeholders.
- When a student hacks an assessment, study the bypass and improve the task rather than pretending it did not happen.

## 5. Reading scores and helping students

- High marks, time spent, traces and real learning transfer are different observations; do not equate them.
- For grade analysis, distinguish retries, latest saved versus best, before-deadline versus late, absent versus missing.
- Question packaging and difficulty distort inferred mastery; one historical nine-skill model predicted worse than a question-mean baseline.
- Poor backend or deployment marks may come from ports, auth, cloud billing, tooling and uptime, not missing domain understanding.
- The loud or active student community is a biased sample; seek evidence from absent and struggling students too.
- Liveliness and extra effort are useful only if they produce learning, not merely memorable or lengthy gameplay.
- Teach learners to debug their AI interaction: task specification, context, checking and interpretation, rather than repeating the model output.
- Assessment traces can inform research, but use appropriate privacy, consent, aggregation and access controls.
- For a TDS solution, identify the deliverable, allowed tools, required environment, deadline and grading contract before prescribing implementation.
- Choose agents and tools by cost, latency, reliability and verification trade-offs, not an assumed best brand.
- Faculty knowledge is provisional; student solver breakthroughs and new capabilities can force course changes.
